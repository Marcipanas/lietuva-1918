# Architecture

Lietuva 1918 is twenty classic scripts sharing one global scope, a stylesheet, and three.js drawing into one canvas. There is no build step. The world has no grid: roads are curves, buildings sit at any angle. This document explains how the pieces fit, so a change in one place does not break another.

## Layers

```
library          vendor/three.min.js                       three.js r159 (global THREE), used only by the view
data             common.js  data.js  geo.js  land.js        constants, rules, geography in lon/lat (geo.js generated by tools/geo)
geometry         geom.js                                    points, polylines, rotated boxes, spatial hash
terrain          terrain.js                                 hidden raster of terrain and soils, path planner
world            world.js  world1918.js                     road and rail graph, buildings, lots, analysis, 1918 map (generated once)
simulation       sim.js  history.js                         state S, daily tick, routes, people, saving; trade abroad, timeline, cards, plan
view (3D)        models.js  scene.js  agents.js             models, renderer, people and vehicles
interaction      tools.js  input.js  panels.js  dash.js     the tools and their plans, gestures and keys, sheet content, dashboards
                 tutorial.js  ui.js                         first-time hints; top bar, navigation, sheet, action bar, actions, loop, boot
```

`common` to `history` never touch the DOM, the canvas or THREE, and run headless in `tools/smoke-test.js`. The view reads the world and never changes it. The world tells the view what changed through version counters (`WV.net`, `WV.bld`, `WV.lots`, `WV.own`) and one hook, `terrainHook(cells)`, called when forest is cleared or deposits change.

## Units and coordinates

Everything is in km on a flat plane: `x` east, `z` south, from the corner at 20.75°E, 56.55°N. The country is drawn `MAPK` = 2 times its real size (decision 38), so the map is `MAPU` × `MAPV` = 800 × 620 drawn km for the real 400 × 310. In three.js, `y` is up and the ground is `y = 0`.

Rules that span the country are set in real km and scaled by `MAPK` in data.js: `TERRITORY`, `WALK_KM`, `HALL_GAP`, `VIL_OFF`, `TRIP_OVERHEAD`, `BORDER_KM`, line costs and upkeep per km (divided after `ROADT`/`RAILT`), vehicle speeds (multiplied after `VT`), lake radii (land.js), soils and forest patterns (`toKm`, the coarse noise in `genTerrain`), the planner's step limit, and the view's country-wide thresholds (camera range, town and map labels, political colours). Rules inside a town keep their drawn size: `ACCESS`, `POL_R`, lots, footprints, the drawn width of rivers and roads. The player sees real km: `kmTxt(x)` turns a drawn distance into real km.

| Conversion | Function |
|---|---|
| lon/lat → km | `ll2xz(lon, lat)`; back with `xz2ll(x, z)` |
| km → raster cell | `cellAt(x, z)` → `k`; cell centre `cellX(k)`, `cellZ(k)` |
| screen → ground | `groundAt(sx, sy)` (ray onto `y = 0`), `toScreen(x, y, z)` |

Town scale is exaggerated about five times (a house lot is `LOT_W` = 0.42 km), so towns are visible on a country-sized map; on the map drawn twice its size there is room between them. Models in models.js are written at twice their drawn size and scaled by `MS` = 0.5 in `MB.geometry()` and `model()`.

**Rotated boxes.** Buildings and lots are `{x, z, rot, w, d}`: centre, rotation like three.js `rotation.y`, width along local x, depth along local z. Local +z is the front, the side that faces the road; its world direction is `(sin rot, cos rot)`. `faceRot(dx, dz)` gives the rotation that turns a front toward a direction. `geom.js` has the box tests (`obbOverlap`, `obbSegDist`, `obbPolyDist`, `obbHas`, `toLocal`, `toWorld`).

## Terrain (terrain.js)

A hidden raster of `CELL` = 0.5 km cells (`GW` × `GH` = 1600 × 1240) holds:

| Array | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `TT` | terrain: `T_FOREIGN`, `T_LAND`, `T_SEA`, `T_LAGOON`, `T_LAKE`, `T_RIVER`, `T_SAND` |
| `TF` | bit flags: `F_FOREST`, `F_FERTILE`, `F_BOG`, `F_CLAY`, `F_FLAX`, `F_KR` (Klaipėda Region, as the 1918 map was built), `F_LT` (inside today's Lithuania) |
| `CTY` | country of each cell today, 0 for water: 1 Lithuania, 2 Latvia, 3 Belarus, 4 Poland's Suwałki region, 5 Russia (Kaliningrad), 6 Masuria (Poland's land that was East Prussia until 1945); `CTY_KEY` names its key in `S.own` |
| `RGN` | part of today's Lithuania that changed hands (`RGN_KEY`): 0 none, 1 the Klaipėda Region, 2 the Palanga coast, 3 the Vilnius region that came back in 1939, 4 the part that came in 1940 |
| `TF0` | `TF` as generated, so saves store only the differences (`tfDiff`, `tfApply`) |
| `BLK` | what is built on each cell, for the planner only: 1 building, 2 road, 4 railway, 8 zoned lot (`blk()` in world.js rebuilds it lazily) |

`genTerrain()` unpacks the raster the start map carries (`WORLD1918.ras`: a palette of cell values and runs, packed by `rasPack` in `npm run world`; about 10 ms), or, when building the start map, rasterizes `geo.js` and `land.js` (about 2 s); after that it only resets `TF`. Generating: water polygons first, then each country's land and Masuria cut from Poland (`GEO_EP`; cells that simplified outlines leave out take a neighbour's value), the Klaipėda Region and the other regions (`RGN`, from outlines widened a kilometre past today's border so no cell along it is missed), sand within 1 km of the open sea, rivers stamped at half their width (at least 0.26 km; the thin Danė and Vilnia are drawn only), lake outlines and small lake ellipses, then soils and forests with noisy edges. Rules sample the raster (building needs, bridges, forests); the player never sees cells. The 3D ground is drawn from it as a smooth texture.

The raster is too coarse for the drawn rivers and lakes, so `waterHit(box, pad)` tests a footprint against the exact river lines (a spatial hash of segments with their half widths) and lake outlines. Lots, buildings, town streets and town halls use it.

**Geography data.** `js/geo.js` is generated by `tools/geo` (see its README): OpenStreetMap borders, Natural Earth coast, lakes and rivers bent onto the towns that stand on them, the Klaipėda Region (`GEO_KR`, and `GEO_KRW` widened for the raster), the Palanga coast and the two parts of the Vilnius region (`GEO_PAL`, `GEO_V39`, `GEO_V40`, built from today's municipalities and elderships), Masuria (`GEO_EP`, today's Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship, whose border with Podlaskie is the old border of East Prussia), and `PLACES`, every town and village from Euronym. Outlines are stored as steps of 1/10000 degree (`geoRing`). `js/land.js` is hand-drawn: soils, forests, deposits, small lakes, map labels in Lithuanian (`MAP_LBL`), and the 1918 map data below.

**Who holds the land.** `ownerOf(k)` gives a cell's holder from `S.own`: for Lithuanian land the holder of its region (or `'lt'`), for a neighbour's land the holder of that country's land. `heldOff(k)` is true for a cell of today's Lithuania that someone else holds: nothing is built or planned there.

**Path planning.** `planPath(ax, az, bx, bz, mode)` runs A* over the cells (8 directions, `planCost`: water, buildings and land others hold block, rivers cost `BRIDGE` × 1.4, a line of the same kind costs 0.35 so new lines reuse it), (`PL.reuse`; 0.75 while the start map is generated, so dirt roads do not wander along each other), then `straighten` replaces cell steps with straight segments where `lineCheck` says the line is clear and crosses no more river. `lineCheck` also asks the world for an exact building test (`exactBlock`). `planTrace(trace, mode)` is what the road tool uses: it simplifies the finger trace (`rdp`), keeps straight pieces that are clear, plans around obstacles where not, and smooths corners (`chaikin`).

## World (world.js)

State lives in `S` (see below); world.js keeps lookups: `NODES`, `EDGES`, `BMAP`, `LOTS`, and spatial hashes `EH` (edge segments), `BH` (buildings), `LH` (lots), updated incrementally.

**The graph.** Two graphs share one node and edge list: `e.k` is `'road'` or `'rail'`. An edge is `{id, k, lv, a, b, p}` with `p` a polyline from node `a` to node `b` (densified to ≤ 0.8 km segments) and runtime `c` (cumulative lengths) and `L` (length).
- `buildLine(k, lv, pts, snapR)` attaches both ends to the network (`snapNet`: an existing node, or a point on an edge, which `splitEdge` turns into a node), finds where the new line crosses edges of the same kind and splits both there, then adds the pieces. Roads and railways cross each other on the level.
- `newRuns(pts, k)`: the parts of a planned line that do not already run along a line of the same kind. Only these are built and paid for.
- `removeEdge` drops lone nodes and joins two edges that meet at a node with nothing else (`mergeNode`), never across a region's line.
- `splitAtRegions()` splits every edge where it crosses from one part of Lithuania into another (ignoring crossings under 0.35 km), so each edge lies in one part (`edgeRgn(e)`, cached as `e.rg`). It runs on load and whenever land changes hands. `analyze()` then marks edges in land others hold `e.off`: Dijkstra, `reachGood`, `accessOf`, the groups, the rail network and upkeep all skip them, so nothing travels through that land.
- `afterLine(edges)` clears trees and zoned lots under new lines.

**Buildings and lots.** `addB(t, x, z, rot, lv, quiet, fp)` (quiet: do not mark the network dirty, used for houses; fp: a footprint of its own, used by landmarks), `removeB`. Landmarks (`t: 'landmark'`, or a hall with `lm`) carry `b.lm`, a key of `LANDMARKS`; they cost nothing and cannot be removed. `validPlace(t, x, z, rot, gen)` checks the cells under the footprint (inside Lithuania, dry land, not held by others: `heldTxt` says by whom), overlap with buildings and lines, and the building's need (forest, bog, clay, open land, rail, coast, lake, shore, river, border). House lots in villages are farmsteads (`b.f = 1`, `FARMSTEAD_CAP` = 0.5k residents; `houseCap(b)` in sim.js). Zoned lots are boxes along streets: `lotSpots(edge)` lists the candidates on both sides, `zonePlan(trace, R)` picks the free ones near a brush stroke, `bullPlan(trace, R)` collects what a removal stroke touches (edges are cut only where touched).

**Network analysis (`analyze()`).** Runs when `NET.dirty` is set. With 451 settlements it takes about 60 ms in Node (`accessOf` skips segments whose nearest point is beyond reach before the exact box test).
1. Each active town hall's road access point (`accessOf`: nearest edge within `ACCESS` + 0.15 of the footprint).
2. Two multi-source Dijkstra sweeps over road nodes from all halls (`dijkstra`, `distTo`): `NET.R0` with plain distances, and `NET.R` where each village starts `VIL_OFF` (6 km) behind. `ownerAt(e, s, pref)` gives the owner and the true distance of any road point. Homes and zoned lots use `R0` (the nearest settlement); workplaces use `R`, so they join a town unless a village is much nearer. Anything beyond `TERRITORY` (15 km) gets `b.na` = `far`; no road access gives `noroad`; in land others hold gives `locked`.
3. Land bonuses: forestry counts forest within 1.6 km; farms and flax fields check fertile or flax soil under them. Border posts get the market across the border of the day (`b.gate = borderOf(x, z)`: the holder of the nearest land others hold within 3.5 km, `OWN_GATE`).
4. Groups of settlements by union-find. `NET.comp`: joined by any road or railway, so routes can run between them. `NET.grid`: joined by railways and paved roads only (a settlement joins every railway or paved node within `R × 1.6 + 1.5` km of its hall by road, and its stations); this is also the power grid. `NET.linked`: the capital's `grid` group, which goals count. `NET.rail`: towns with a station on the capital's railway network, for the railway tasks.
5. Neighbours: where the catchments of two towns meet along a road (`R0`) or a railway (a sweep from all stations), they are neighbours, and the meeting gives the exact length between them (`NET.edges` = `{a, b, road: {len}, rail: {len}}`, `NET.adj`). Commuting and the Neighbours list use these.
6. Line lengths and upkeep (`NET.km`, `NET.up`), pollution sources (`NET.pol`, read with `polAt(x, z)`).

Town-to-town paths are not computed here. `pairPath(k, a, b)` runs a Dijkstra from one town that stops as soon as nothing can beat the best way to the other, and keeps the result `{len, lv (weakest level), path}` until `NET.ver` changes. Routes, vehicles, route highlights and the Neighbours list call it.

**The 1918 map (`genWorld`).** Run once by `tools/build-world.js` (`npm run world`, about 18 s) and stored in `js/world1918.js` in the save format; `newGame()` loads it (`loadStartMap`) and only falls back to `genWorld()` without it. `tools/check.js` fails when the files it was made from have changed (`tools/world-hash.js`). The steps:
1. `settlements()`: every place in `PLACES` at its real site, with the game's details from `TOWNS0` and `TOWNS1` matched by name. Other towns get a size from today's population (1 to 3k) and a line of text from their region and river (`townText`, or `TOWN_TXT`); villages get 0.4k to 1.2k from their Euronym rank and soil.
2. Landmarks at their real places (`LANDMARKS`), first, so streets go around them. Vilnius and Kaunas town halls are landmarks.
3. The five big cities (`CITY_PLAN`, `layoutCity`): the town hall, the real main streets as paved roads, then whole blocks of a street grid 0.95 km apart turned by the plan's angle, enough for the city's homes, nearest the hall first and grown outward from the blocks the main streets run through. A block is taken only when it is dry and has no landmark, so its sides run corner to corner; a side along a main street is left to it. Streets cut off by water, and grid streets left with a loose end, are dropped. Other towns: `layoutTown` (a market square, curving streets, ring roads). Villages: `layoutVillage` (a street, a chapel, farmsteads). Streets avoid rivers, lakes and buildings (`clearRuns`).
4. Railways of 1918 (`RAIL1918`, station by station; `@lon,lat` runs on to the border): `stationFor` (at `CITY_PLAN.station` for the cities), `railBetween`, `railToBorder`; where a line meets another, `turnouts` curves it onto the old track at a shallow angle, as a switch does (the Rail tool does the same). Paved highways (`ROAD1918`) the same way at level 2, leaving cities by their paved streets (`townExit` with `lv` 2).
5. Dirt roads (`linkSettlements`): the relative neighbourhood graph of all settlements, shortest pairs first, skipping a pair already joined by a road at most 1.35 × the straight line plus 1.5 km (`roadDist`); then any settlement still cut off from Vilnius tries its nearest neighbours (`roadComps`). Then `BORDER_ROADS`.
6. Homes for towns and cities (`settleTown`, only along roads their hall reaches), and the starting industries of `TOWNS0` (`placeNear`, which takes a spot only if its road leads to the town without a long way round: the Klaipėda port stands on the city side, not on the Curonian Spit).
7. `tidyStreets`: loose ends that stop within 0.4 km of another street are joined to it; roads with a loose end that no building or lot uses (by their road access) are removed, again and again; a street that runs on past the last building or lot it serves is cut back to 0.35 km beyond them. Roads to the border stay.
Planned lines go through `buildPlanned`: points near a line of the same kind are moved onto it, and only the new runs (`newRuns`) are built, so lines never run doubled. `foundTown` lays out a town the player founds.

## State (`S` in sim.js)

```
S = { day, funds, inc, era, speed, own{ kr, pal, vr, vr2 (parts of Lithuania), lv, by, pl, ep, ru (neighbours' land): holder }, nid, rid, gid, lid, did,
      prices{res}, log[ {d, t, k, yo (year only)} ] (every 'hist' line kept, other news trimmed to 150), won, ms{}, mod{farm}, ev{event id: 1}, nextRnd, obj{}, stats{portSales, routesMade},
      towns[ {id, n, x, z, R (radius), capital, vil, kr, orig, lock, pop, pop0 (1918 size), h, stock{}, dl{}, dem{}, cap (storage), rg (its region, not saved), …per-tick fields} ],
      bld[ {id, t, x, z, rot, lv, on, town, cd, k, eff, why, f? (farmstead), gate? (border post), ti? (halls and gen stations), lm? fp? (landmarks)} ],
      routes[ {id, a, b, mode, vt, n, ab, ba, pax, …per-tick fields} ],
      nodes[ {id, x, z} ], edges[ {id, k, lv, a, b, p} ], lots[ {id, x, z, rot, town, cd} ],
      deals[ {id, m (market), r, dir ('out' | 'in'), q (loads a day), ti (gateway town), …per-tick fields} ],
      mkt{ de, gb, lv, pl, su: {open, f (price factor), why} },
      hist{ path ('free' | 'soviet' | 'neutral'), sec, def (army Lt a day), ally, trust, exp{}, war, purge, collect, mood[], cards[] (waiting), spd, picks{card: option}, risk{card: 1 worked, 0 failed}, unl{}, proj[], plans[], sup, union, mark, bases, … },
      plan{ y, name, tgt{res}, got{res} } or null,
      cam, sel, tab }
```

**Land and its holders.** `S.own` starts as `startOwners()` (16 February 1918). `setOwner(key, holder)` hands a part of Lithuania or a neighbour's land over: it splits edges at region lines, locks or unlocks that region's settlements (keeping what they had; on return at least their 1918 size), drops their deals, moves the capital (`fixCapital`: Kaunas while others hold Vilnius, and while `S.hist.kaunas` says the government sits there, from January 1919 until `seatVilnius()`) and returns what changed hands for the Chronicle. Holders are `lt`, `lv`, `pl`, `de`, `al` (the Allies), `ra` (the Red Army, 1919 and 1920), `by` and `ru`; at the start German troops hold all the neighbours' land. `settleOwners()` sets each town's region (`t.rg`) and lock on load. `landShare()` scales the eras' residents and linked towns, and the goal, by the share of the 1918 residents and towns living in the land held now, against the land held at the start.

**Saves** are kept under `SAVE_KEY`, which carries the version: a new version starts a new game, and `dropOldSaves()` removes older saves on start. No code reads them.

**A new game** (`newGame`) loads the start map, tops up homes where a town starts with fewer than its people (`homesAtStart`: some city houses sit nearer a village hall), adds the routes of 1918, and runs one day of figures without moving the date, so the dashboards have something to show. It starts paused with `S.intro` set: the interface shows the intro card until the player begins. `S.eraCard` holds a new era's card the same way.

**News for the screen.** Simulation code has no DOM, so it pushes short texts to `NOTES` (sim.js): finished ministry tasks, population milestones, an empty treasury, and the first sentence of history events that change the map or the markets. The interface shows them as toasts, one at a time, when no card is open. Warnings about food and cold collect in `WARN` during a day and go to the Chronicle as one line each (`warnLog`).

**Ministry tasks** (`OBJ`) run from easy to hard; `can()` says whether a task makes sense now, and `curTask()` gives the first open one that does, for the map.

Saves and the start map share one compact form (`encWorld`, `decWorld`): nodes and lots as arrays rounded to 10 m, edges with their lines as steps from the previous point (straight runs simplified away with `rdp`, densified again on load), buildings as short arrays (`encB`), and the terrain flags as a difference from generation. `serialize()` also leaves out what each day recomputes (`dem`, `ate`, and the dashboards' `mk`, `us`, `gr`), empty stock, and the 1918 descriptions, which `restore()` takes back from the start map; numbers are rounded to 4 decimals (`SAVE_NUM`). A save is about 510 KB, mostly the 451 settlements and their roads.

## The day tick (`tick()` in sim.js)

1. Prices drift toward their base (times the Depression factor, `depAt`). `BYT` groups buildings by town. Unconnected buildings get `eff = 0` and a reason.
2. `phase1`: housing (`houseCap`), jobs, storage (`STORE_BASE` plus warehouses), schools, clinics, pollution at homes (`polAt`).
3. `routeEval` for every route: path from `pairPath`, capacity each way per day `n · cap · speed · roadFactor / (2 · km + TRIP_OVERHEAD)`.
4. `commute`: local jobs first, then walking to neighbours up to `WALK_KM` (30 km) by road, then routes that carry commuters.
5. `phase1b`: education, health, productivity, power plants. Then the power grid per group of linked towns (villages need no power).
6. `phase2`: production (× `histProd()`: war, inspectors), the settlement's own fields and woodlots (`pop0`, × `histSub` for collective farms), food basket and variety, heating, linen and electronics demand, morale (+ `histMood`), growth, taxes (× `histTax`), building upkeep. What a town makes and uses today goes into `t._mk` and `t._us` (inputs, food, fuel, goods, materials for homes).
7. `runRoutes`: `moveCargo` each way; auto cargo moves a good only toward the town with fewer days of supply, and a store's surplus (over 75% full) toward a town with room. `runTraders`: private merchants on each pair of neighbouring towns (`NET.edges`, whose `road.lv` is the kind of road where the towns' lands meet) close a twentieth of the gap between plenty and need a day, at most `TRADE_CAP` loads (1.5 by rail); `TRADES` keeps the day's flows for the carts. Then `runDeals` (history.js): exports and imports through gateway towns (keeping `d.lim` of the store), and deliveries to the Union.
8. `develop`: towns with full homes, fed and content people build on their best zoned lots every 5 days, or rebuild old houses in a later era.
9. Smoothed daily figures per town: `dl` (net change of each good, routes and trade included), `mk` (made), `us` (used), `tr` (merchants' net flow), `gr` (residents gained).
10. Line and vehicle upkeep and `histSpend` (army, allies, support schemes), then the day's balance: `S.inc` and, by kind, `S.bud` (from `BUD`: taxes, trade, new homes, building upkeep, lines, route vehicles, army). Then `histTick` (history.js): news cards for history that moves a border or the capital (`queueNews`, events with `news`),  dated events (`HEV`, each with an `id` kept in `S.ev` once passed; `when` decides on the day whether it applies), decision cards (`queueCard`, which pauses the game; `when2` decides whether the card comes; `pickCard(n)` resolves one; `decisions()` lists them for the Chronicle), army spending and security, farm support, yearly security from trade with Britain, the yearly state plan (`closePlan`, `openPlan`), Union projects. Then random events (`REV`), ministry tasks (`OBJ`), milestones, the win check. Every 7 days `S.series` gets `[day, residents, treasury]` for the trend lines (halved in resolution past 520 samples).

## The 3D view

**models.js** builds low-poly geometry with vertex colours from boxes, gable and hip roofs, cylinders, cones, lathes and blobs (`MB`, the mesh builder, with a push/pop local transform). `MODEL_FN` holds one function per building type returning `{geo, h, smoke, fields, flag, cows, pigs, boat}`. Houses are small parts placed by `lotLayout(b)` (three or four wooden houses and a shed, a farmstead with a barn, brick blocks, or panel blocks), tinted per instance. Village halls are drawn as a wooden chapel (`modelKey`), border posts as a customs house with a barrier, and landmarks by their own functions (`LM_*`, `MODEL_FN['lm_' + key]`), about twice life size so they stand among the houses. Trees, people, animals and vehicles have their own small geometries.

**scene.js**:
- Renderer, `SCENE`, perspective camera `CAM3` on an orbit rig `cam = {x, z, dist, yaw, pitch}` (`applyCam`, `panBy`, `zoomAt`, `rotateBy`, `tiltBy`, `focusAt`, `northUp`). Hemisphere light plus a sun whose shadow box follows the view (updates stop beyond 70 km).
- Snow is a shader patch (`snowy(material)`): every upward-facing surface whitens with `SNOW_U` (times `userData.snowMul` per material).
- Political view: `POLTEX` holds each cell's holder colour and whether others hold it (alpha), painted with the farmland mask when `WV.own` changes (`paintPol`). `POL_U` rises from 0 at 90 km to 1 at 230 km; the ground shader then mixes land others hold toward its holder's colour and lifts its snow, keeps three quarters of Lithuania's snow, and the shapes beyond the map edge drop their snow. Up close, snow on land others hold is a third lighter. The ground has its own material (`groundMat`): base texture painted from the raster (`paintGround`, `patchGround` for small changes), a per-pixel patchwork of fields where the mask allows (`MASK`, `FARM_PAL` by season), fading out when fields get smaller than a pixel, then patchy snow.
- Water: polygons for the sea (run on past the data's west and north edges), the lagoon and lakes, ribbons for rivers at their own widths; lakes and rivers freeze in deep winter.
- Holders: each holder of land has its own muted colour (`OWN_COL`; Soviet holders share one), in the ground texture inside the map and as flat shapes beyond it (`outerColours`; Masuria's shape lies over Poland's with a polygon offset). Land of today's Lithuania that others hold is washed in its holder's colour (`cellRGB`). The ground is repainted when `WV.own` changes.
- Borders (`buildBorder`): today's borders (`GEO_BORD`; a Polish side is the Suwałki region or Masuria by the cell there), the region lines (`GEO_KRW`, `GEO_PAL`, `GEO_V39`, `GEO_V40`; sides found up to a kilometre out, so winding lines keep their pieces) and the old border of East Prussia (`GEO_EP` where it meets the Suwałki region) are resampled once into even half-kilometre pieces that know the land on either side (`borderPieces`); a piece within 0.3 km of another line's piece is dropped, so shared stretches are drawn once. A piece is drawn where the two sides have different holders: Lithuania's border of the day dashed and dark, the others' thinner and lighter. Pieces that follow on from each other are joined, so each dash bends with the line. Rebuilt when `WV.own` changes or the zoom changes width class. The field patchwork (`paintMask`) is fainter on land others hold, as on the neighbours' land, so the holder's tint shows up close; it repaints when `WV.own` changes.
- Map labels name the neighbours, the sea and the lagoon, and towns abroad in Lithuanian; the neighbours' names follow their holders (`nlText`: a second line says who occupies them, as Vokietijos užimta in 1918; the Belarusian side reads LENKIJA between the wars, Masuria names its holder, East Prussia reads KALININGRADO SRITIS from 1946), and each part of Lithuania others hold shows its name and holder (MAŽOJI LIETUVA or KLAIPĖDOS KRAŠTAS, VIDURIO LIETUVA or VILNIAUS KRAŠTAS).
- Roads and railways (`buildLines`): one ribbon mesh per kind and level with a repeating canvas texture, junction discs up close (under 45 km), bridges where they cross rivers (found once per network version, `bridgeRuns`). Rebuilt when `WV.net` changes or the zoom changes width class (lines widen when far out so they stay visible; dirt roads less, since they are everywhere).
- Buildings, houses, fields, flags (`buildBuildings`) and lots (`buildLots`) are `InstancedMesh`es grouped by model, managed by `Pool`. Only buildings inside the view box (plus a margin) are added, and the pools are refilled when the camera moves past a grid step (`bldView`). Beyond 40 km, each house lot is one simple block (`far`, `farmFar` parts); once the map turns political (180 km), house lots are not drawn and the town labels stand for them.
- Trees (`updateTrees`) are built per 20 km chunk when first in view, shuffled so that drawing only the first part thins the forest with distance; birches change colour in autumn and lose their crowns in winter.
- Smoke particles (`SMOKE`, `puff`) from chimneys, kilns, power stations, cooling towers and steam engines; cows, pigs and boats (`updateCritters`).
- Overlays (`buildOverlays`): a planned line in pieces (`VIEW.lines`: new stretches amber, old ones it upgrades green, ones it only uses thin) with a ring at each of its points, the building in the way (`VIEW.hit`), brush ring, zoning and removal previews, ghost building with its fields and pollution ring, route highlight, selection ring, pollution discs. Rebuilt when `VIEW.ver` or the world changes. While a building is being placed, `updateNeed()` lights the ground that suits it (clay, bog, forest, fertile soil, the flax belt) with screen-space hatching (`NEED_TEX`, `NEED_MAT`).
- View offset: panels cover the bottom of the view on phones, so `CAM3.setViewOffset` shifts the picture up by `VOFF.y` pixels (eased toward `VOFF.ty`, which ui.js sets to half the covered height). The camera's target then sits in the middle of what stays visible, and `groundAt`, `toScreen` and picking stay exact.
- Labels (`updateLabels`): town names, map labels and problem markers are HTML elements in `#labels`, moved every frame. A town's dot is red for an urgent problem, amber when something holds it back, green when it thrives (`townAlert` in dash.js; villages show only red). Cities always show their names, towns of 4k and more up to 230 km camera distance, smaller towns up to 120 km, villages up to 38 km (`labelZoom`).
- House lots take the style of their place (`lotStyle`, worked out once per lot and not saved): plastered tenements of two or three storeys wall to wall along the street, with courtyard wings, in the inner three quarters of a big city ('c'); two masonry houses on a market square near a town's hall ('m'); single-storey wooden town houses with their gable ends to the street ('t'); farmsteads with a log troba under thatch, a granary (klėtis) with its porch, a barn, a well sweep, fruit trees, a fence and sometimes a roadside cross ('v'). Walls and roofs are separate instanced parts tinted from `PART_PAL`; window frames and trim are parts of their own so they stay white. Far out a lot is one part (`cityFar`, `townFar`, `farmFar`).
- Production view (`VIEW.prod`, switched by `prodBar` in ui.js): `prodMarks` puts a marker with the good's icon over each building that makes goods (dimmed when idle) up to 32 km out, and further out each town's main goods with what its buildings make a day (`townProd`, recomputed once a day).
- `pickBuilding(sx, sy)`: ray against every nearby footprint box.

**agents.js**: residents walk between homes and work along street paths (`streetPath` runs a Dijkstra), commuters walk between neighbouring towns, route vehicles shuttle along `pairPath` paths, and a merchant's cart (or train) runs on each of the 24 busiest trading links of the day (`TVEH`, from `TRADES`, refreshed every 6 s). All are instanced; they scale up when zoomed out so they stay visible.

## Interaction

The interface is built for a phone first. A slim status bar sits on top (date, treasury, play or pause, speed). The map fills the rest. At the bottom, a bar with the five views (Build, Routes, Lithuania, Trade or Plan, Chronicle) opens the **sheet**: a bottom sheet on phones that snaps closed, half or full (drag its handle, or tap it), and a side panel beside the map on wide screens (960 px and up). Tapping a town or a building opens its card in the same sheet. While a tool is in use, an **action bar** takes the place of the five views: what the tool will do, what it costs and who pays, and the buttons (Undo, Turn, Find a spot, Build or Zone or Remove, close).

- **input.js**: gestures and keys. One finger always moves the map; two fingers pinch, twist and pan together, or tilt when both slide up or down. With a tool, a tap acts; holding still for 320 ms and then dragging draws a line freehand or paints (`holdStart`); a drag that starts on the ghost building or on a point of a planned line moves it (`hitGhost`, `hitHandle`), and the map moves along near the edges (`edgePan`). Mouse: wheel zooms at the cursor, right-drag turns and tilts, the ghost follows the pointer and a click on it builds.
- **tools.js**: the tools in world km, so tests drive them directly. A ready action fills `PEND`, which `showBar()` (ui.js) shows and `doConfirm()` applies.
  - Road and Railway: the line is a list of tapped points (`TRACE`; `addPoint`, `undoStep`, `moveHandle`). An end on the network is snapped (`snapEnd`; a tap on a town's middle means its streets) and leaves the network where it faces the next point, clear of houses (`exitPoint`). `planTrace` (terrain.js) plans through the points on 0.5 km cells; `routePieces` then samples the plan, replaces stretches that follow lines of the same kind by the true way along the network (`netPath`, a bounded Dijkstra), and keeps the rest as new stretches joined to the network where the join misses every building (`joinPoint`). New stretches are built (`buildLine`); old ones of a lower kind than the chosen one (`TOOL.lv`, `lineLevel`) are upgraded (`upSpans`, `applyUps`, which split edges covered in part). A town at either end that has the materials pays (`linePayer`). The first tap on an old line of a lower kind also offers to upgrade that whole stretch (`prepareUpgrade`).
  - Zone: each tap or stroke adds the lots along the streets it touches (`ZONES`, `addZone`); Undo takes the last one back. Remove: a tap takes one thing, a stroke everything it touches (`bullPlan`).
  - A building placed where no road reaches it (`PEND.acc` false) gets its road laid out in the Road tool right after (`offerAccess`: a dirt road from the building's back or side to the nearest street of the town that would take it, with `TOOL.free0` so its first point stays at the building), for the player to confirm.
  - Build: `setTool('place', type, town)` puts the ghost on the best spot with `findSpot`: places that face a road (a railway for a station) near the town, nearest first, where the ground suits the building (`quickNeed`, with masks of cells near the border, the coast, lakes and water, `nearMask`) and a town takes it in; it looks further out (45, 90, 160, 260 km) when nothing is near. The ghost snaps to the nearest road and faces it (`setGhost`); Turn rotates it.
- **panels.js**: the sheet's HTML as strings: its header (`sheetHead`), the build menu (`buildPanel`: lines and land, then categories, then the buildings of one), the town card (`townPanel`: summary, six health tiles, problems with fixes, goods made and used, buildings, neighbours, stock), the building card, routes, the country view (`natPanel`: trend tiles, problems by kind with the places that have them, goods, towns sorted by need, goal, era, tasks, security), trade or the plan, and the chronicle. Whatever changes while the sheet is open goes through `v()` (text), `w()` (bar width), `pd()` (chart path) or `sc()` (a state such as good, warn or bad), so the sheet patches values in place.
- **dash.js**: the analysis behind the dashboards, with no DOM. `townIssues(ti)` lists a settlement's problems, worst first, each with one-tap fixes: a route from the neighbour with the most to spare (`suggestRoute`), the building that makes what is missing (`PRODUCER`), zoning, a warehouse, selling. `natIssues()` groups them for the country (kept for the day), `goodsRows(ti)` gives goods made, used and stocked for a town or the country, `sparkPath()` draws the trend lines from `S.series`.
- **ui.js**: the status bar and speed (`updateTop`, `setSpeed`), the navigation (`renderNav`, `navTo`, with badges for urgent problems and new chronicle entries), the sheet (`setSheet`, drag with `sheetDown`/`sheetMove`/`sheetUp`, `closeSheet`), selection, `renderPanel(force)` (patches values when the structure is unchanged, rebuilds it at most every 1.5 s otherwise, and skips the work while the sheet is closed), the action bar (`showBar`), `act(dataset)` for every `data-act` button (taps that only change the view do not save), `frame()` (simulation at `TPS[speed]` days a second, stopping while a decision card waits; then `VOFF.ty = coverBottom() / 2`, agents and the view), `showCard()` for decision cards, and `start()`.

## Saving and offline

- Autosave to `localStorage[SAVE_KEY]` every 30 days but at most every 20 seconds (`autosave`), on hide and after actions.
- `sw.js` precaches every shipped file, including `vendor/three.min.js`. Bump `VERSION` on every release.

## Performance budget

Measured headless (Node): a simulation tick takes about 8 ms with 451 settlements, `analyze()` about 60 ms, a new game about 0.2 s after the terrain (0.8 s, once per session). The scene is 35 to 55 draw calls and 100k to 250k triangles depending on zoom; the 7,400 km of dirt roads are most of it when zoomed out. Keep it that way:
- Add things as instances in an existing `Pool`, not as separate meshes.
- Anything numerous needs a distance limit or a view box (buildings: `bldView`; trees: `TREE_MAX` 55 km and chunk level of detail; walkers: 11 km; vehicles: 70 km; critters: 12 km; village labels: 38 km).
- Rebuild meshes only when a version counter changes or the camera crosses a grid step, never every frame.
- `analyze()` runs after every build action; keep it linear in the size of the graph (no all-pairs work).
